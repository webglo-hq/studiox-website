How it works

  1. 1

    Agents prepare

    Thirteen skills run in calendar order: bank and card reconciliation, accruals, prepaids, depreciation, subledger tie-outs, intercompany, FX, eliminations, journal review, flux and the close package. They read through typed tools and can only propose.

  2. 2

    Ontology checks verify

    Every proposal is checked against a formal close ontology before anyone sees it: balanced, postable accounts, the right period, counterparties on intercompany lines, matched amounts, evidence cited. Failed checks block; nothing is waved through.

  3. 3

    People approve

    Your autonomy policy (L0 to L3, plus rules in plain English like "Never auto-post anything over $5,000") applies the routine. The rest lands in one inbox with the reason, confidence and evidence, routed to someone whose limit covers it.

Controls you can show an auditor

Agents are fast. The controls are what make them usable. Bookhand enforces the important ones in the database itself, so a bug or a clever prompt cannot route around them.

  • Maker-checker everywhere. The database refuses an approval by whoever prepared the entry or started the agent run behind it; reconciliations, flux reviews and checklist tasks are signed off by a second person.
  • Nobody grants themselves power. A role with approval authority, or a change to a control, takes effect only when an independent Controller or CFO approves it. Administrators never see anyone's password: people enrol with a single-use link sent to them.
  • Approval limits and SoD rules. Per-role limits; segregation-of-duties rules that block or warn, recorded on the approval.
  • Posted means posted. Balanced entries, immutable once posted, no postings into locked periods. Undo is a reversal.
  • Tamper-evident audit log. Every change by a person, agent or connector, hash-chained and verifiable in one click.
  • Prompt-injection hygiene. Bank memos and vendor text are data, never instructions; anything that reads like an instruction is flagged and never auto-applied.
  • Assistants act as you, not as admin. Claude and other MCP clients use a personal access token with your roles and limits, recorded as "via MCP".

Questions controllers ask

Will the agents post to my books without asking?

Only what your policy allows: verified items under your limits, with no risk flags, at the autonomy level you set for that skill (L0 suggests only; L1 always asks). Auto-applied work is sampled for after-the-fact review, and every item can be reversed.

Which AI does it use?

Claude through Anthropic's API when you add a key; Opus by default, Sonnet or Haiku if you prefer. Without a key, a deterministic engine runs the same skills. Either way every proposal passes the same checks and approvals.

Where does our data live?

In your PostgreSQL database. The model sees only what a skill's typed tools return for the task at hand.

What does our auditor get?

A read-only seat: every proposal with its evidence and checks, every approval and sign-off, and an audit chain they can verify themselves.

Can I change what the agents do?

Yes. Each skill has a policy you edit in plain English and thresholds you set; each change is a new version with a reason, written to the audit log.
