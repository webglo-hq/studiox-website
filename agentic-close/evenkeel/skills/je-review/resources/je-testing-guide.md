# Journal entry testing guide

Auditing standards (PCAOB AS 2401.58–.62) require testing journal entries for management
override. Characteristics that raise risk:

| Test | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Same person prepared and approved | Segregation of duties is missing — one person can record anything |
| Weekend or late-night posting | Outside normal process and review |
| Round amounts (e.g. $48,000.00) | Estimates or plugs rather than transaction-driven amounts |
| Manual entries to control accounts | Bypass the subledger and its controls |
| Just under an approval threshold | Possible structuring to avoid review |
| Unusual account pairs | Combinations never seen before (e.g. revenue against receivables with no invoice) |
| Duplicate payments | Same vendor, same amount, close dates |
| Benford's law | First-digit distribution far from expected can indicate fabricated amounts (Nigrini 2012) |

Wording: state the fact, the risk, the assertion and the next step. Example: "ERP-58213
($48,000 revenue true-up) was prepared and approved by d.mercer and posted Saturday 22:14 PT
without an invoice. Risk: revenue occurrence. Next: obtain support from the CFO's office;
reverse if none."
