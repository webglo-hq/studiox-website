How it works

Four steps from open month to locked books.

  1. 1

    Agents prepare

    An orchestrator runs each close task in dependency order: coding, bank and subledger reconciliations, accruals, prepaids, depreciation, intercompany, FX revaluation and journal-entry review.

  2. 2

    You review the why

    Every proposal shows the agent's reason, its confidence, the evidence and exactly what will post. Approve in bulk or one at a time; rejecting needs a reason.

  3. 3

    Certify and explain

    Preparers certify reconciliations that tie to zero; a different person reviews them. Material variances get explanations that someone other than the author approves.

  4. 4

    Sign and lock

    The CFO signs a close package whose numbers are hashed. The controller locks the month, and the ledger then refuses any posting dated inside it.

Controls your auditor can test

Built so that speed never costs you control.

Maker-checker in the database

Whoever an agent works for cannot approve its work. Posted entries are immutable and corrected only by mirror reversals. PostgreSQL enforces both, not just the screen.

Autonomy you set, with ceilings

Choose per skill: suggest, draft for approval, auto-post under a threshold, or auto-post with review after. Material items and anything that failed a check always wait for a person.

Every proposal explains itself

A plain-language reason, a confidence, the evidence and deterministic checks: balanced, postable accounts, open period, ties to source. Arithmetic is done by code, not by the model.

A tamper-evident trail

Every change by a person or an agent is chained by hash, and edits or deletions are refused. Each agent run keeps its plan, tool calls, checks, cost and decisions.

Bank text is data, never instructions

Statement memos and documents are treated as untrusted. Text that tries to instruct the agent is flagged, routed to a person and never auto-posted.

Evals you can rerun

Every skill ships with test cases that run against a full close in CI. The skill catalog shows the latest results, instead of an accuracy claim you have to trust.

Questions

What people ask first.

Does the AI post entries on its own?

Only where you allow it. At L2 an agent may post verified, confident items under the amount you set, and a sample is reviewed afterwards; at L3 every auto-posted item is reviewed. Material items, anything that failed a check and anything with suspicious source text always wait for a person.

Which model does it use?

Claude, through the Anthropic API with your key, in three tiers chosen per skill: Haiku for fast work, Sonnet for most tasks and Opus for deep review. Without a key, a deterministic engine runs the same tools and controls, which is how the demo works.

What can our auditors see?

Everything, read-only: every entry with its provenance, every agent run with its trace, every approval and sign-off, and an audit log they can verify end to end.

Does it replace our ERP?

No. Evenkeel keeps its own double-entry ledger for the close and imports bank and subledger data (CSV, BAI2, OFX and ISO 20022 camt.053) with provenance and idempotent imports.

Where does our data live?

In your PostgreSQL 16 database. Nothing leaves it except the model calls you turn on.
